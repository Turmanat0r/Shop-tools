A fuse protects the wire, not the device. It has to be equal to or smaller than the wire's ampacity, and comfortably larger than the continuous load. LED drivers pull a brief inrush surge at switch-on that a fast-blow fuse sitting right at the load current can nuisance-trip on — that's why the 80% rule exists.

A shared ground carries every device's current at once, so it has to be sized for the sum, not for one pod. Undersized grounds are the most common cause of dim, flickering, or oddly-behaving lights — the power side gets sized carefully and the return gets whatever scrap was on the bench. Run the ground at least as heavy as the feed. A bolted chassis ground only works if the paint is off and the joint stays clean; if there's any doubt, run a real wire back to the battery negative.

Gauge Ladder — tap to select

Green clears your drop limit and has the ampacity. Amber carries the current but exceeds the drop limit. Red is under-rated for the current — don't run it.

Resistance is solid copper at 20°C, matching the AWG standard to better than 0.05%. Stranded runs about 2% higher and warm wire higher again, so treat the drop as a floor and leave margin. Ampacity follows ABYC-style DC values outside an engine space with up to three bundled conductors — derate for engine bays, big bundles, or high ambient.
